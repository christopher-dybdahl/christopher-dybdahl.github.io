Optimal Make–Take Contracts between an Exchange and a Market Maker

Market Microstructure project, Imperial College London, 2026 · with T. Delforge and J. Jareño García

Report (PDF) Code

How should an exchange design make–take fees when its market maker also internalises flow in a dark pool? Following Baldacci, Manziuk, Mastrolia and Rosenbaum (2019), this is a principal–agent problem: the exchange (principal) posts an incentive contract over the market maker's executed volumes; the market maker (agent) responds with optimal bid and ask quotes in both the lit book and the dark pool, managing inventory risk. The contract that emerges rewards liquidity provision asymmetrically in inventory — paying the market maker to quote on the side that reduces its imbalance.

A nested stochastic control problem

The agent's optimal quotes maximise a Hamiltonian over four controls (bid/ask in lit and dark); given the agent's best response, the principal's value function solves its own HJB equation over the space of incentive schedules. We solve the nested problem with neural networks end-to-end:

  • Agent best response. A neural network \(L(z, q)\) trained to maximise the continuous-time Hamiltonian pointwise over quotes, as a function of the incentive \(z\) and inventory \(q\).
  • Principal's HJB. A backward-in-time actor–critic sweep: at each time step the critic approximates the exchange's value function and the actor outputs the incentive schedule, with the critic target advanced by a Heun predictor–corrector scheme and a stochastic exploration loss stabilising the actor.

$$ V^{E}(t, q) \;=\; \sup_{z}\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \,\text{fees} - \text{incentive payments} \;\middle|\; \text{MM best response } L^{*}(z, q) \right], $$

solved backwards from the terminal date one time step at a time, each step a supervised learning problem.

Results

  • The learned incentive schedules are monotone in inventory and steeper in the dark pool — the exchange pays most for liquidity exactly where adverse selection is highest.
  • Optimal quoted volumes and the resulting fee revenue were computed across three parameter regimes (tick size, dark-pool intensity, inventory aversion).
  • The report also analyses Hawkes-driven order flow and outlines a deep BSDE formulation as an alternative solver for the principal's equation.

Implementation note: the repository's current solver uses a parallel (Jacobi-style) update over time steps with a Heun critic target; the report's headline runs used a sequential backward sweep at finer time resolution. Both variants are described in the repository README.
