Optimal Make–Take Contracts between an Exchange and a Market Maker

Market Microstructure project, Imperial College London, 2026 · with T. Delforge and J. Jareño García

Report (PDF) Code

How should an exchange design make–take fees when its market maker also internalises flow in a dark pool? Following Baldacci, Manziuk, Mastrolia and Rosenbaum (2019), this is a principal–agent problem: the exchange posts an incentive contract over the market maker's executed volumes, and the market maker responds with optimal bid and ask quotes in the lit book and the dark pool while managing inventory risk. Neither of the two nested value functions has a usable closed form, so we solved the whole problem with neural networks. Methodologically this is the same programme as my NTNU thesis: deep learning as the numerical method for high-dimensional Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations that classical solvers cannot handle well.

Two coupled learning problems

The structure of the control problem maps directly onto two neural training loops:

  • The agent's best response, learned without labels. A network \(L(z, q)\) takes the incentive \(z\) and inventory \(q\) and outputs the four quotes (bid and ask, lit and dark). It is trained by maximising the continuous-time Hamiltonian directly through the computational graph: the economic objective itself is the training signal, so no solved examples are ever needed.
  • The principal's HJB, solved as supervised learning. Backward induction turns the exchange's PDE into a sequence of regression problems, one per time step, with an actor–critic split: the critic approximates the exchange's value function and the actor outputs the incentive schedule. The critic's regression target is advanced in time by a Heun predictor–corrector step, and a stochastic exploration loss keeps the actor from collapsing onto degenerate contracts early in training.

$$ V^{E}(t, q) \;=\; \sup_{z}\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \,\text{fees} - \text{incentive payments} \;\middle|\; \text{MM best response } L^{*}(z, q) \right], $$

solved backwards from the terminal date one time step at a time, each step a supervised learning problem. The repository implements two solver regimes: a sequential backward sweep, which is more accurate per time step, and a parallel Jacobi-style update over all time steps at once, which trades some accuracy for much cheaper iterations. Choosing between them is a standard compute-versus-accuracy decision rather than a modelling one.

Results

  • The learned incentive schedules are monotone in inventory and steeper in the dark pool: the exchange pays most for liquidity exactly where adverse selection is highest.
  • Optimal quoted volumes and the resulting fee revenue were computed across three parameter regimes (tick size, dark-pool intensity, inventory aversion).
  • The report also analyses Hawkes-driven order flow and outlines a deep BSDE formulation as an alternative solver for the principal's equation.

Implementation note: the repository's current solver uses the parallel Jacobi-style update with a Heun critic target; the report's headline runs used the sequential backward sweep at finer time resolution. Both variants are described in the repository README.
