Combining Alpha Signals under Governance Constraints

Forecast Aggregation, Estimation, and Evaluation over Unknown Populations · MSc thesis, Imperial College London, September 2026

A systematic trading book runs several forecasting models on a shared universe of assets. The coefficients that combine their signals into per-asset forecasts are constrained in practice from two directions: by the investment process, which needs attribution, deliberate sizing and bounded change, and by estimation, which cannot support the unrestricted regression that is optimal when the population is known. The thesis measures both sides of that trade-off.

Approach

  • With signals and returns standardised, the portfolio problem reduces to correlations, and the transfer coefficient (the fraction of the maximal Sharpe ratio a construction attains) prices any combination matrix exactly.
  • For staged constructions, an exact two-factor decomposition separates the cost of an aggregation structure from the cost of the weights used within it.
  • Because the true population is unknown, estimated combination rules are evaluated as decision rules over an explicit prior on populations, with an uninformed equal-weight benchmark, comparisons confined to governance classes, and every conclusion re-examined under deliberate distortions of the prior.

Findings

  • Structure is cheap; weights are expensive. With known parameters, per-asset blending costs one to two percent of the maximal Sharpe ratio, while equal weights cost fifteen to thirty-seven percent, almost all of it information destroyed by equal-weight aggregation itself.
  • Under estimation, the ordering follows parameter count. Model-level rules improve on the benchmark from a year of data in most drawn worlds; full per-asset estimation needs years; the unrestricted regression is never competitive.
  • The strongest feasible rule pools estimated skill towards per-model averages with an intensity that can be stated rather than estimated; the safest rules anchor or gate movement away from equal weights and hold the benchmark even under the prior distortion that most favours them.

The thesis and accompanying codebase are not public at this time; happy to discuss the work in detail.
